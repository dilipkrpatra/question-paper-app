Strategic sourcing is a systematic, data-driven approach to deciding what to buy, from whom to buy it, under what commercial terms, and how to manage the supplier relationship in order to achieve the best overall value for the organization.
The Strategic Sourcing Process

A typical strategic sourcing cycle looks like this:

1. Spend Analysis: Understand what the company is buying, from whom, volumes, prices and historical spending.
2. Requirement Analysis: Understand exactly what the business needs—specifications, volumes, quality, service levels, delivery requirements, etc.
3. Supply Market Analysis: Study the supplier market, available technologies, capacity, competition, cost drivers and supply risks.
4. Sourcing Strategy: Decide the appropriate approach—for example:
Single vs. multiple suppliers, Local vs. global sourcing, Make vs. buy, Long-term vs. short-term contract, Competitive bidding vs. negotiation
5. Supplier Identification & Qualification: Identify potential suppliers and evaluate their capability, financial strength, quality, capacity, technology and risk.
6. RFQ/RFP & Evaluation: Invite suppliers to submit commercial and technical proposals and evaluate those using defined criteria.
7. Negotiation: Negotiate price, payment terms, lead time, MOQ, warranty, quality, logistics, escalation clauses, service levels, etc.
8. Supplier Selection & Contracting: Select the supplier(s), finalize the commercial agreement and establish the contract.
9. Supplier Performance Management: Monitor cost, quality, delivery, service, innovation and risk.
10. Continuous Improvement: Look for further savings, process improvements, supplier development and risk reduction.
